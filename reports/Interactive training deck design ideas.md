# Borrowing the best e-learning ideas for NOVIKONTAS decks

The factory already matches most of what the e-learning world publishes. Its 13 slide templates, 19 answer types and 10 interactive types, the 2D and 3D kits, the step runner and the motion law cover every block that Articulate Rise, Genially and the classroom tools offer. The research found no published product that combines an instructor-projected deck, trainee tablets, 3D equipment kits and graded tasks, so there is nothing to copy wholesale. What it does show is **a short list of gaps, most of them small**. They are: hazard colours and colour-blind-safe feedback, figure and body text that is too small for a projector, lining numerals for gauge values, real-photo hazard tasks, a before/after slider, a "predict first" pause slide, and visual links between a term in the text and its part in the figure. The large idea, showing every tablet's answers on the projector, comes from Nearpod and Mentimeter and needs the training app's backend. The sourced facts are in sections 2 and 3, and what we already do is in section 1. Proposals are in section 4. The ranked list of changes is in section 5. Evidence for maritime competitors is thin: public material is marketing text, so "what their screens look like" is largely unknown.

## 1. Where the factory already matches best practice

Rise 360 lists about a dozen interactive blocks: accordion, tabs, labeled graphic, process, scenario, sorting, timeline, flashcards, a "Continue" gate, and statement callouts ([Articulate](https://www.articulatesupport.com/de/article/Rise-Lesson-and-Block-Types)). Adobe's 2026 guide names six recurring patterns: drag-and-drop matching, scenario questions, branching with consequences, video hotspots, simulations and role-play ([Adobe](https://elearning.adobe.com/2026/01/how-to-design-interactive-e-learning-experiences-that-truly-engage-modern-learners/)). Set against our baseline, almost all of these exist. We have content-in-parts tabs, hotspot, label_diagram, order, match, categorise, a scenario that shows the consequence, and a step runner that works as a gate. Our `.takeaway` and `.law` blocks are a small fixed set of callouts, which is what the Statement A-D idea in Rise amounts to.

The instructional-animation research also lines up with our rules. Animation beats still pictures only modestly overall (Höffler & Leutner d = 0.37; later reviews g ≈ 0.23), but it is clearly better when the learner must learn the specifics of a change ([Leibniz IPN](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis); [Springer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w)). Our law L13/L20 already says motion must explain a mechanism, flow or sequence, and that decorative motion is rejected (`course-visuals/review/knowledge/motion-rules.json`). Mayer's principles, with median effects from about 0.4 to 0.9, point the same way. Labels sit on the part (spatial contiguity), decoration is cut (coherence), and animations are segmented by Next presses (segmenting, d ≈ 0.79) ([Cambridge Handbook](https://cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-managing-essential-processing-in-multimedia-learning-segmenting-pretraining-and-modality-principles/DD24C2F48B9B1277CE59F78276110258)). Sketchfab's numbered pins with Next/Previous camera flights ([Sketchfab](https://help.sketchfab.com/en/articles/16152328)) are what our 3D kit already does, with a 750 ms tween driven by the deck's Next/Back. Our slide entrance of 0.38 s sits in the 250-400 ms decelerate range that Material-style motion tokens suggest ([M3 tokens, community port](https://docs.rs/crate/hydrolysis-m3/latest/source/src/theme/motion/tokens.rs)). The L62 rule (no two adjacent slides alike, at least 80% light) matches the advice against a template look. The baseline's own caveat applies throughout: it is built from specs and CSS, and no built deck was inspected in a browser.

## 2. Sourced facts: what comparable products do

**Authoring tools and classroom tools.** Articulate's challenge recaps show the same patterns recurring across hundreds of examples: tabs, before/after comparisons, image sliders, gate screens, virtual tours, branching scenarios and results slides ([Articulate Community](https://community.articulate.com/category/e-learning-challenges-and-recaps); fetch-blocked, titles only). Genially adds interactive images, infographics and escape-room games ([Genially](https://elearningindustry.com/directory/elearning-software/genially)). In the classroom tools, Mentimeter offers 12-13 response types, including pin-on-image, ranking and 2x2 grid; Pear Deck and Nearpod run teacher-paced sessions, and Nearpod also lets the teacher lock screens, set timers and use 3D objects and simulations ([FLT Mag](https://fltmag.com/interactive-slides-comparison/)). Curipod lets the instructor freeze a slide and add or take 30 seconds. The consistent feature is that the instructor controls pace and sees the group's answers.

**HTML slide frameworks.** reveal.js fragments cover fade, highlight and "current-only" steps, and auto-animate morphs matched elements between two slides ([reveal.js fragments](https://revealjs.com/fragments/); [auto-animate](https://revealjs.com/auto-animate/)). Slidev ties every animation to a click, shows presenter notes and a timer, and morphs code between steps ([Slidev](https://sli.dev/guide/animations)). Their value is an object that persists across steps, not decorative transitions.

**Maritime and industrial.** Seagull/Videotel (now OTG) invested in 3D animation and gaming overlays on video ([Splash247](https://splash247.com/seagull-videotel-under-new-management/)). Seably carries KARCO's 3D-animated courses that explain tricky processes ([Maritime Executive](https://www.maritime-executive.com/article/seably-introduces-karco-3d-animated-online-marine-courses)). Kongsberg's dual-fuel LNG bunkering simulator offers a 3D walkthrough, an instructor station with live alarms, **recording and replay for debrief, and automated assessment** for unsupervised runs ([Kongsberg](https://kongsbergmaritime.com/feature_articles/2025/11/preparing-the-lng-workforce-for-a-cleaner-future/)); its LNG carrier model splits junior tasks (rounds) from senior tasks (emergencies) ([Kongsberg L22](https://kongsbergmaritime.com/products/simulation/k-sim-engine/k-sim-engine-l22-man-me-gi-lng-carrier/)). Maersk warns that VR "can't compensate for weak learning objectives" ([Maersk Training](https://maersktraining.com/news-and-insights/news/virtual-reality-hype-reality-and-value-in-maritime-and-offshore-training)). Lockout/tagout and hazard products use realistic scenes: find the hidden hazards, pick the right device, isolate in order, graded ([Vector Solutions](https://www.vectorsolutions.com/courses/safety-challenge-spot-the-hidden-hazards/); [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/etools/lockout-tagout); [FreeRangeXR](https://pixovr.com/vr-training-content/lock-out-tag-out-freerangexr/)).

**Explainers.** Bartosz Ciechanowski's articles are the best-documented model of a clear technical explainer. Each named part keeps a fixed colour that the text refers to; parts are added one at a time; cut-aways are marked with diagonal hatching and surrounding parts hide; one slider drives one variable and a linked plot follows; there is a global pause; and a credit-card outline gives scale ([Mechanical Watch](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/); [Internal Combustion Engine](https://ciechanow.ski/internal-combustion-engine/); [Naval Architecture](https://ciechanow.ski/naval-architecture/)). The Distill guide to interactive articles adds prediction prompts before the answer, multiple representations of one idea, and details on demand ([Distill](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)).

## 3. Sourced facts: format, legibility and colour

**Slide density.** In two studies with 110 and 111 students, sentence headlines with visual evidence ("assertion-evidence") gave better comprehension of complex concepts and fewer misconceptions than topic-and-bullet slides ([Garner & Alley, IJEE](https://www.ijee.ie/articles/Vol29-6/23_ijee2791ns.pdf); [ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/17510)). The effect is modest, a follow-up was not statistically significant, and the quality of the visual evidence mattered most ([Penn State](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/high-quality-visual-evidence-on-presentation-slides-may-offset-th-2/)). Bare numbers are recalled better from common-practice slides in the short term, so key values belong in the figure label. Duarte's glance test asks for the main point in three seconds ([Duarte](https://www.duarte.com/resources/slide-glance-test/)).

**Projector legibility.** The audio-visual "8H rule" says the farthest viewer sits no more than eight screen heights away, and minimum text height is about 1/50 of screen height, doubled for a long room; it has no primary study behind it ([PPT FAQ](https://www.pptfaq.com/FAQ00125_How_big_should_text_be-_What-s_the_ideal_font_size-.htm)). Stricter guides use 1/25 of height, or 24-32 pt body with 20 pt as an absolute minimum ([UW-Parkside](https://www.uwp.edu/explore/offices/campustechnologyservices/innovationsinlearning/upload/2010-08-13_Slide_Present_Tips_ETTE.pdf)). On a 1080 px slide, with 1 pt = 2 px (my arithmetic), that is **40 px minimum and 48-64 px body**. Dark text on a light ground reads better for normal vision, but nothing was found on projectors, so polarity should follow room light and a measured contrast check ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)). WCAG gives 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons and state ([WebAIM](https://webaim.org/articles/contrast/)).

**Colour.** ISO 3864 gives red/white for prohibition and fire equipment, yellow/black for hazard, blue/white for mandatory action and green/white for safe condition; ANSI Z535 adds orange for warning; hex values differ between editions ([ISO 3864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3864); [InCompliance](https://incompliancemag.com/ansi-z535-1-safety-colors-in-focus/)). The Okabe-Ito palette is the colour-blind-safe reference set, and about 8% of men have a colour-vision deficiency ([NYU Siegal](https://siegal.bio.nyu.edu/color-palette/)).

**Tablet tasks.** WCAG 2.2 sets 24 px as the minimum target and 44 px as AAA; Material uses 48 dp and Apple 44 pt ([TetraLogical](https://tetralogical.com/blog/2022/12/20/foundations-target-size/)). Explanatory feedback (what, how, why) beats verification-only feedback, and immediate feedback suits hard tasks and novices ([Shute 2008](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654307313795), secondary summaries). Our controls are already 56 px and wrong answers already give a hint and a brief reason, so we meet this.

## 4. Proposals: comparing the research with the known weaknesses

Everything here is my judgement. The tablet and projector feasibility is reasoned from the baseline, not measured.

**Too dark, and pastel drawings with tiny labels.** The 80/20 light-dark rule answers the darkness complaint, and the polarity evidence supports it. The labels are the larger open issue. The kit grows label type until it reaches a floor of 12.5 px (`kit/SPEC.md:33-49`; `floor.json`). On a 1080 px projector that is about 1/86 of the screen height. Even if the stage is scaled up from 1280x800 (about 17 px), it is below the 22 px that the loosest 1/50 rule gives, and far below the 40 px of the university guides. A tablet read at arm's length tolerates 12.5 px; a back row does not. I propose a separate **projected floor** for figures: 22 px absolute, 32 px as the target for labels, with the kit's collision-free placement doing the work of fitting fewer, larger labels. That also means choosing fewer labels per view, which the 3D kit already limits to 3-9. For body text, our `--lt-fs` is clamp(15, 2.5vmin, 30), which reaches 27 px at 1080 and is capped at 30. That clears the 1/50 minimum but not the 1/25 guide (43 px). Raising the body cap toward 40 px costs words per slide, which fits the assertion-evidence evidence (fewer words, stronger visual) but needs a decision, because the current budget is 85 words beside a picture against the roughly 25-40 words those sources suggest. Before changing anything, someone should stand in the back row of a Novikontas classroom and read a deck. For the pale labels, add a 7:1 target for main projected text and 4.5:1 for secondary text to the audits, since the sources only guarantee 4.5:1 and ambient light lifts a projector's black level (my proposal, unsourced).

**No hazard colours.** The token set has none, and the owner decision is open (`tokens.json:180-182`). ISO 3864 and ANSI Z535 give a ready meaning table. I propose a small hazard set used only in its safety sense (prohibition red, hazard yellow, mandatory blue, safe green), each always paired with an icon shape and a word, and applied to `.warnbox`, `spot_the_hazard` and fire/gas figure parts. Our own right/wrong tokens are green #12805A and red #B4453A, a red-green pair that colour-vision-deficient trainees may not tell apart. The research suggests blue/orange for feedback plus a tick or cross and the words "right" and "not yet". Whether the existing `.q-opt.right/.wrong` already carry icons was not visible in the baseline; check first.

**Few real photos in tasks.** The industrial-safety products are almost entirely realistic scenes with a hazard hunt followed by an ordered action. Our `spot_the_hazard`, `hotspot` and `order` types already express that, so the missing piece is photos and a standard template, not a new mechanism. I propose a "find, then isolate in order" task template that requires a real photograph, an enlarged invisible hit area of at least 48 dp around each hotspot, and a build audit that counts photo-based tasks per module (the full-build review found only 7 in the whole course). Photo supply, not code, is the limit. Spaces such as the tank connection space exist only as drawings, so each of these needs a photo at Novikontas or a licensed image.

**Low learner-active time (M01 at 32% against an 80% target).** Adobe's rule is to tie each interaction to an objective and place a check after each key section. Distill adds prediction before reveal. We have the `predict` type but no standard moment for it. I propose a recurring **"predict first" pause slide**: after a teaching slide, one tap question on the tablets (where does the gas go first, which valve opens first), then the figure plays the answer. It is cheap, it fits Next-driven pacing, and it adds active time without a new engine.

**Missing kit parts and explainer techniques.** Ciechanowski's clearest ideas are partly present: our 3D kit has cut-aways and ghost walls, and the hatched cut-aways and real colours are already queued in the round-2 handoff. New items are colour-per-part with text to match, hide-the-rest, assembly by steps and a linked plot. Because red and amber carry hazard meaning, text-to-part links should use a coloured **numbered marker** (the numbered `.legend` and label dots already exist) rather than free colours. A slider that drives a flow with a small linked chart already has its parts (`scale`, `curve_chart`) and needs only wiring. Two further techniques are marked low priority: a scale reference and a realistic-versus-exaggerated toggle.

**Instructor interaction.** The strongest copyable feature of Nearpod and Mentimeter is the aggregate answer shown on the projector. Our model already matches theirs (instructor paces, tablets answer), but we show no group result. This needs a channel between tablets and the projecting device, and the training app has a backend, so it is feasible but large, and I did not find any source on the architecture. A cheaper version of the Kongsberg replay is a time-stamped log of trainee taps for the instructor's debrief. I do not propose copying timers, since our task rules say no timers, or gamification badges, which the notes judged of doubtful value for accredited training.

**Numerals.** Raleway uses old-style numerals by default, where digits sit at different heights. That is general font knowledge, not a sourced finding, and I did not confirm that our bundle sets lining figures. For gauge readings, pressures and set-points, `font-feature-settings: "lnum"` is a one-line fix to check.

## Conclusion

The research mostly confirms the factory's direction. The new value is in the unglamorous items: a projector-sized floor for figure text, a hazard colour set, a colour-blind-safe pair for right and wrong, and photo-based hazard tasks. None needs a new engine and all fit on the tablet. The biggest uncertainty is whether the classroom projector is the real constraint. The 12.5 px floor was set by tablet measurements, and the only projector number in the baseline is a test size. One back-row reading test would settle changes 4, 5 and 16.

Three limits apply. The comparison with maritime competitors rests on marketing text, and no competitor screens were seen. Articulate's example pages could not be opened. The learning-science effect sizes come mostly from individual screen learning, so how well they hold for an instructor-narrated projected class is not established. Treat items 15 and 16 as owner decisions, because they trade words per slide against legibility.
