Warmer cargo, higher pressure

A liquefied gas boils until its vapour reaches the pressure for its temperature. Propane at 20 °C needs a tank that holds about 8 bar; cooled to -42 °C it is held at atmospheric pressure.

The high-level trip

Loading slows to top off. If the level still climbs, the high-level alarm sounds - and the independent high-high level sensor trips the ESD, which stops the loading.

Why a tank is inerted before air goes in

Methane burns only in a narrow zone of mixtures. Air blown straight into a tank of methane passes through it; inert gas first leaves so little methane that the air never reaches it. Drag how much methane is left when the air goes in.

Methane's limits in air (5 - 15 %) are standard; the zone's lower tip (about 12 % oxygen) is drawn approximately, not yet checked against the course's sources.

A leak at the manifold

Discharging at night, a gas detector alarms at the manifold. Minute by minute: the ESD stops the cargo, water spray knocks the gas cloud down, the crew musters upwind.
